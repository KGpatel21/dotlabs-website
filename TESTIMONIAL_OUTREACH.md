# 🗣️ Testimonial Outreach Kit — start today

Goal: replace the placeholder quote WORDS on the site with real client words.
The attribution format (role · industry · country) stays — no names needed.

## Step 1 — List your past freelance clients (5 minutes)

Open a note and list every client since 2019 you're on good terms with.
For each: name · project · their role · industry · country · best channel
(WhatsApp / email).

## Step 2 — Send the ask (copy-paste)

### WhatsApp version (Indian clients / informal)

> Hi [name] 👋 Hope you're doing great! Quick update — I've registered my
> freelance work as a company now: **Sparken Technologies**
> (sparkentechnologies.com). I'm adding a few short client quotes to the
> site. Could you share 1–2 lines about how it was working with me on
> [project]? I'll show it anonymously as just "[Role], [Industry], [Country]"
> — no name or company. If you're busy, I can draft something from your past
> feedback and you just approve it 🙏

### Email version (formal / international)

> Subject: Quick favour — 2 lines about our work together?
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I've formalised my freelance practice into a company — Sparken Technologies
> (sparkentechnologies.com). I'm collecting a few short client quotes for the
> website.
>
> Would you be open to sharing 1–2 sentences about our work on [project]?
> It would be shown anonymously as "[Your role], [industry], [country]" —
> no company name needed.
>
> If it's easier, I can draft something based on your past feedback for you
> to simply approve or edit.
>
> Thanks so much,
> Krutarth · Sparken Technologies

### The "draft for approval" trick (use it — doubles your response rate)

Most clients are happy but lazy. Send a SECOND message with a pre-written
quote based on what they actually said during the project:

> Here's a draft — feel free to edit or just reply "approved":
> "Krutarth delivered exactly what he scoped, on time, and explained every
> technical decision in plain language. We've come back to him twice since."

They reply "approved" → that's a real, usable quote.

## Step 3 — Follow up once (after 4–5 days)

> Hey [name], just bumping this — even one line helps a lot. And if you'd
> rather skip it, totally fine 🙂

One follow-up only. Never pressure.

## Rules (protects you)

- Never invent a quote. Never edit meaning — only grammar, with approval.
- Anonymous attribution is fine and industry-standard for NDA'd work.
- A named quote (with permission) is even stronger — ask only if natural.
- Bonus ask, same message: "Would you also mind a Google review?" → send
  your Google Business Profile review link once verified.
